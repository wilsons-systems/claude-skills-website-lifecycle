# Brand Assessment — Wilsons Systems

**Client:** Wilsons Systems / Wilsons Electrical Ltd
**Skill applied:** Brand Voice + Creative Direction
**Date:** May 2026

---

## Brand Foundation — As-Is

### Current Identity

Wilsons Systems operates on a dark visual theme: black backgrounds, red accents, white type. This is an earned look for a contractor — it reads as technical, confident, and premium without being flashy. It differentiates from the sea of white-background local trade websites. The direction is correct. The execution needs tightening.

**What the dark theme communicates (correctly):**
- We take our work seriously
- We are not the cheapest option
- We work on sophisticated systems, not just standard domestic installs

**What it currently lacks:**
- A consistent typographic system (no documented font stack)
- Human warmth — no team photos, no faces, no story
- Structured credential display — NICEIC and Control4 badges exist but are not featured prominently as trust anchors

### Logo

The current logo has not been supplied in vector format. A high-resolution SVG version is required before any web rebuild or printed materials. The logo should be available in:

- Full-colour version (for use on dark backgrounds — current primary)
- Reversed / white version (for use on light backgrounds if needed)
- Monochrome version (for embroidery, single-colour print)
- Minimum size: legible at 120px wide on mobile screens

**Action required:** commission or locate an SVG version of the existing mark. If the original file no longer exists, a vector trace from the current PNG is a 1–2 hour design task.

### Typeface

No typeface is documented. This needs to be formalised before any new pages are designed or copy is set.

**Recommendation:**

| Role | Font | Why |
|------|------|-----|
| Headings | Inter Bold or Barlow Condensed Bold | Clean, modern, technical — works well reversed on dark |
| Body | Inter Regular | Highly legible at small sizes, excellent web rendering, Google Fonts (free) |
| Accent / badge labels | Inter Medium | Consistent with body, slight weight step |

Both Inter and Barlow are available on Google Fonts at no cost and load fast via the Google Fonts API. Inter in particular has become the benchmark for technical professional services.

### Positioning — Currently Implicit

The business has never formally articulated its market position. This is the current implicit positioning, assembled from what the business actually does and what its accreditations represent:

> *"The Fylde Coast's complete electrical and smart home contractor — the only NICEIC-approved, Control4-authorised team that can handle everything from a landlord's EICR to a full smart home installation, backed by 40 years of local knowledge."*

This is not currently stated anywhere on the website. It should be.

**One-sentence positioning statement (for internal use — not verbatim on the site):**

> Wilsons Systems is the multi-discipline trade contractor that serious Fylde Coast homeowners and developers reach for when they want one accountable team to do it all properly.

## Visual Direction Assessment — 4-Axis Framework

### Axis 1: Tone
**Position: Professional with warmth**

Not corporate-cold. Not mate-down-the-pub casual. The tone sits firmly in the "trusted local expert" register — the person who turns up when they say they will, explains what they're doing without condescending, and gives you a straight answer about cost. Think: the consultant GP, not the NHS waiting room poster.

Visual implication: the dark palette is correct, but it needs a human layer. Real photographs of Ryan, Jon, and the team — on site, in workwear, with tools — shift the tone from "anonymous contractor website" to "people you're letting into your house."

### Axis 2: Aesthetic
**Position: Polished Standard**

Not editorial-restrained (art gallery minimal would alienate the domestic electrical customer who needs reassurance, not serenity). Not maximalist (busy layouts with too many elements signal a budget trade site). Polished Standard means: clean, structured, plenty of white (or dark equivalent) space, accreditation logos treated as genuine brand marks, photography that looks professional but not staged.

For the dark theme specifically: use a true near-black (#0f0f0f or #111) for backgrounds, not a flat #000. Red accent (#cc1414 or similar — check current brand hex) should appear sparingly — for CTAs, hover states, and selected navigation states. Not as borders around every element.

### Axis 3: Audience Relationship
**Position: Expert-to-trusted-neighbour**

Wilsons is talking to people who don't always know exactly what they need. The homeowner searching "EICR Blackpool" might not know what an EICR is. The landlord searching "electrical certificate" just knows they need one by law. The Lytham homeowner searching "Control4 installer" might not know the difference between Control4 and a cheap Amazon smart switch.

The design must support a voice that says: *we know exactly what you need, we'll tell you straight, and we've been doing this for 40 years.* This means:
- Use plain language for headings ("Get a landlord electrical certificate" not "EICR Compliance Services")
- Explain what things cost, at least in ranges — it builds trust
- Use outcome-led page structures ("Here's what you get, here's what it costs, here's how to book")

### Axis 4: Sensory Ambition
**Position: Confident Clarity**

The goal is not to impress with visual complexity. The goal is to make the customer feel certain they're dealing with professionals. Confident clarity means:
- Accreditation badges visible on every page — header or footer strip, not hidden on an About page
- Before-and-after photography where available (consumer unit replacements, cable trunking, smart home racks)
- Real team photos in workwear — not stock photography of generic tradespeople
- Completed project photography — Corka Bridge House and similar installs told visually

If photography doesn't yet exist: this is a gap, not a blocker. A half-day photoshoot (team + a completed project) will generate enough material for the whole site launch. Prioritise this before the site goes live.

## Brand Voice Specification

*For use by anyone writing copy for wilsonssystems.com — internal or external.*

### The Three Voice Traits

**1. Straight-talking**
We say what we mean and mean what we say. No hedging, no filler, no corporate padding. If something costs between £300 and £600, we say that. If a job takes a full day, we say that. Customers trust trades who give them straight answers — and distrust those who won't.

**2. Accountable**
We put our name and credentials on everything. NICEIC approval isn't a logo we rent — it means our work is inspected and certified. Control4 authorisation isn't a badge from a website — it means we've been trained, tested, and approved by the manufacturer. We lead with credentials because we've earned them.

**3. Outcome-focused**
We talk about what the customer gets, not what we do. "You'll have a valid landlord electrical certificate, issued within 3 working days" not "We provide EICR services." Every sentence should be written from the customer's side of the job.

### Do / Don't Pairs — 10 Rules for Copy

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| "NICEIC Approved Contractor — your work is certified to the highest standard" | "We are passionate about delivering excellent electrical services" |
| "Get a quote within the hour during business hours" | "Contact us today for a bespoke solution" |
| "40 years on the Fylde Coast — we know these houses" | "Decades of industry experience across multiple verticals" |
| "We're the only Control4 authorised dealer on the Fylde Coast" | "We offer market-leading smart home integration" |
| "You'll get a fixed price before we start" | "We provide competitive and transparent pricing" |
| Write short sentences. Two clauses max per sentence. | Write long, winding sentences with multiple embedded clauses that obscure the point. |
| Use "you" and "your" — address the customer directly | Write in the passive voice: "Inspections are carried out by..." |
| Name the location: "in Blackpool", "across the Fylde Coast", "Lytham St Annes" | Use vague geography: "the local area", "the surrounding region" |
| Give numbers: "13 five-star Google reviews", "40+ years", "3 working days" | Give vague claims: "many satisfied customers", "years of experience", "fast turnaround" |
| Mention the specific technology: "Texecom Premier Elite", "Hikvision", "Control4" | Use generic terms: "advanced alarm systems", "high-quality cameras", "smart technology" |

---

### Banned Phrases — 5 Examples with Replacements

**1. "Bespoke solutions"**
*Why it's banned:* Every trade contractor uses it. It means nothing to the customer. It doesn't describe what you do.
*Replace with:* Describe the specific solution. "We design each Control4 system around how you actually use your home — which rooms, which devices, what you want it to do automatically."

**2. "Passionate about"**
*Why it's banned:* Passion is not a credential. No customer cares whether you love your job. They care whether you do it properly.
*Replace with:* Describe what the passion produces. "We're NICEIC Approved — every installation is inspected and certified." or "We've been doing this for 40 years. The quality speaks for itself."

**3. "State-of-the-art"**
*Why it's banned:* Meaningless. State of the art in 2006 is not the same as 2026. It signals that the writer didn't know what to say.
*Replace with:* Name the product. "We install Hikvision ColorVu cameras — the current benchmark for low-light image quality." or "All our alarm systems use Texecom Premier Elite panels, used by professional security installers across the UK."

**4. "Market-leading"**
*Why it's banned:* Unverifiable. Every contractor claims it. It adds no information.
*Replace with:* State the verifiable fact. "NICEIC Approved Contractor — the UK's most trusted electrical accreditation scheme." or "Control4 is the world's leading home automation system. We're the only authorised installer on the Fylde Coast."

**5. "One-stop shop"**
*Why it's banned:* Informal and worn out. It sounds like a high-street printer, not a professional contractor.
*Replace with:* Name the disciplines. "Electrical, security, CCTV, smart home, fire alarms, and networking — one team, one quote, one point of contact for the whole job."

### Template Opening — Any Service Page

The first two to three sentences of any service page should follow this pattern: establish what the service is (for SEO), say something specific about Wilsons (for trust), and state what the customer gets (for conversion). No preamble, no scene-setting.

**Template pattern:**
> [Service name] in [location], installed by a team with [specific credential or fact]. [What makes Wilsons the right choice for this — one concrete differentiator]. [What the customer gets — outcome, not process].

**Example — EICR page:**
> If you need an Electrical Installation Condition Report in Blackpool or across the Fylde Coast, Wilsons Systems carries out the inspection and issues the certificate — all under our NICEIC Approved Contractor status. We've been working in Blackpool properties for over 40 years, which means fewer surprises on older wiring. You'll have your EICR certificate within 3 working days of the inspection.

**Example — Control4 page:**
> Wilsons Systems is the only Control4 Authorised Dealer on the Fylde Coast. That means we're trained, tested, and approved by Control4 themselves to design, install, and programme their systems — not just to sell them. If you're building or renovating a home in Lytham St Annes, Poulton, or anywhere across Lancashire, we're the team to call.

**Example — CCTV page:**
> CCTV installation in Blackpool and across the Fylde Coast, fitted by a team with over 40 years of local experience. We're Hikvision specialists — one of the UK's most trusted CCTV brands — and we install both domestic and commercial systems. You get a fully configured, remotely viewable system with a 12-month installation warranty.

## What to Keep vs What to Change

### Keep

- **Dark theme** — it works. It differentiates from standard white trade sites. The black/red/white palette signals premium.
- **Premium positioning** — Wilsons is not and should not be the cheapest. The pricing, the accreditations, and the services all support a premium-quality pitch.
- **"40 years" as proof** — this is a first-line trust signal, particularly for domestic customers. Use it in the hero, in meta descriptions, in page intros.
- **NICEIC and Control4 badges** — they should be visible on every page. They are the most powerful trust anchors in the brand toolkit.

### Change

- **The H1** — "About Us" on the homepage is a critical error. Change immediately to "Electrician Blackpool & Fylde Coast | Wilsons Systems" or equivalent.
- **The homepage copy** — the current ~200 words do nothing for SEO or conversion. Replace in full (see File 4).
- **The comment form** — remove from the homepage. It attracts spam and looks unprofessional.
- **The phone number treatment** — it must be click-to-call on mobile, prominent in the header on every page.

### Add

- **A real team photo** — Ryan, Jon, Mat, Ewan in workwear, in front of a van or at a job site. One photo does more for domestic trust than any amount of copy.
- **A consistent accreditation strip** — NICEIC, Control4, Safe Trader, Part P logos in a horizontal bar. Lives in the footer of every page, and optionally in the header or just below the hero.
- **Google reviews widget** — even 13 reviews, displayed live, builds trust. Target 50+ reviews within 6 months (see File 6 for the process).
- **A real About page** — Ryan's story, briefly told. 40 years. Family-run. What multi-discipline capability means for a customer. Two or three team photos.
- **Before/after photography** — consumer unit replacements, alarm panel installs, smart home rack builds. Nothing demonstrates competence like a tidy installation next to a before shot.
