# Brand Assessment — Wilsons Systems

## Summary Verdict

The brand is well-executed. The site is already built and performing. This is not a brand correction exercise — it is a maintenance and extension guide for keeping the brand consistent as new content is added (project write-ups, blog posts, About page updates).

---

## 4-Axis Brand Direction — Confirmed Against the Site

### Tone: Professional with warmth

The existing service page copy strikes the right register. It is confident without being arrogant, knowledgeable without being condescending. The phrase style — "In our experience...", "We recommend..." — positions Wilsons as an expert adviser rather than a vendor. This is correct for the Lytham St Annes audience, who are making significant investments (£5k–£50k+) and expect to be guided, not sold to.

**Confirmed correct. Preserve this tone on all new content.**

### Aesthetic: Polished standard with cinematic dark elements

The visual direction — #111111 background, #C41E2A red, Space Grotesk headings, Plus Jakarta Sans body — matches Control4's own design language deliberately. This is the right call. Control4's Authorised Dealer status is a premium trust signal, and a visual alignment with Control4's aesthetic reinforces that positioning without saying anything directly.

The "dark, cinematic, tech-luxury" direction is not a style affectation — it is a positioning statement. A Lytham homeowner comparing Wilsons against a generic white-background local electrician's website will immediately perceive the difference in quality and intent. That first impression is doing real work.

**Confirmed correct. Do not introduce light-mode designs, bright colours, or generic contractor visual elements in any new pages.**

### Audience relationship: Expert to high-net-worth client

The primary audience is affluent Lytham St Annes homeowners commissioning significant projects. They are not price-sensitive. They do not need to be convinced that electrical work is important — they need to be convinced that Wilsons is the right firm to trust with their home.

This means the copy must treat them as intelligent adults. Do not over-explain basics. Do not reassure them about things they are not worried about. Focus on credentials (NICEIC, 40 years, Control4 Authorised since 2014), real outcomes (named projects, measurable results), and the competence signals that matter to this audience (staff qualifications, process, aftercare).

**Confirmed correct. The Lytham-first positioning is right and must not drift toward Blackpool general-public messaging in any new content.**

### Sensory ambition: Tech-luxury clarity

The site achieves clarity without feeling corporate. The dark aesthetic communicates quality and seriousness without sacrificing readability or warmth. The chatbot, Quick Quote, and Simpro integration signal a business that runs on real systems — not a one-man-band with a brochure site.

**Confirmed correct. Maintain this as new functionality is added.**

## Brand Voice Quick Audit

### What to keep

- **"In our experience..."** — positions Wilsons as a practitioner giving informed guidance, not making marketing claims
- **"We recommend..."** — advisory register, implies the client is getting the benefit of professional judgement
- **British English throughout** — "organised", "recognised", "licence", "authorised" — never American spellings
- **No superlatives** — avoid "best", "leading", "number one", "only". They signal insecurity and create compliance risk. "Authorised Dealer since 2014" is stronger than "the Fylde Coast's best Control4 installer"
- **Service-specific precision** — "Hikvision VASP partner" is better than "CCTV experts". Specificity is a trust signal.

### What to watch for

The 7 sub-service pages (house rewiring, consumer unit upgrades, EICR testing, emergency electrician, heat pump electrician, multi-room audio, lighting control) were likely templated quickly. There is a risk of generic contractor language creeping into these — "We are committed to quality and customer satisfaction"-style filler. When reviewing or adding to these pages, replace any such language with specific, confident, experience-based statements.

Red flags to eliminate:
- "We take pride in..."
- "...to the highest standard"
- "...customer satisfaction is our priority"
- "No job too big or too small"
- "Fully qualified and insured"

Replace with specifics: certifications by name (NICEIC Part P, 18th Edition BS7671), years of experience, named outcomes.

## 5 Do/Don't Pairs for New Content

These apply to all new content: project write-ups, blog/guide posts, About page additions, GBP posts.

**1. Do: Write from experience. Don't: Write from aspiration.**
- Do: "On a recent rewire in Lytham St Annes, we found the consumer unit was original 1970s — we upgraded to a 24-way metal-clad unit and added RCBO protection throughout."
- Don't: "We always strive to deliver the best possible rewiring service for our valued customers."

**2. Do: Name certifications. Don't: Use generic quality claims.**
- Do: "As an NICEIC Approved Contractor and Part P registered firm, we carry full certification."
- Don't: "We are fully qualified and insured professionals."

**3. Do: Acknowledge complexity. Don't: Oversimplify.**
- Do: "Integrating Control4 with existing Lutron dimmers requires careful configuration — here is what that process involves."
- Don't: "We make smart homes simple!"

**4. Do: Let outcomes speak. Don't: Use adjectives about the work.**
- Do: "The Corka Bridge House installation covered 14 zones of audio, full lighting scenes, and integrated gate control — all operated from a single Control4 interface."
- Don't: "We delivered an amazing, seamless smart home experience for a delighted client."

**5. Do: Use British English and full sentences. Don't: Use bullet-point marketing speak.**
- Do: "Our EICR testing service includes a full visual inspection, dead testing of circuits, and a schedule of inspections — all carried out to BS7671:2018+A2:2022."
- Don't: "✓ Fast turnaround ✓ Competitive rates ✓ Fully certified ✓ No hidden fees"

## What Is Genuinely Missing

### Team personality

The About page currently lacks named individuals. For the Lytham St Annes audience in particular — where people buy from people they know and trust — this is a real gap. A named, qualified MD with a photograph is a stronger trust signal than any certification badge.

For E-E-A-T purposes (Google's Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust signals for trades and home service queries), named authors with verifiable qualifications are increasingly important as Google's algorithms have hardened against anonymous contractor sites.

**What to add:**
- Ryan Wilson — role, qualifications (NICEIC Qualified Supervisor, MD), brief background (how long in the trade, what he specialises in)
- Other named team members with qualifications — even first name and cert level is better than nothing
- A photograph of Ryan — this is the single most humanising element a service business About page can have

### Project photography

Several pages likely still use placehold.co placeholder images. For the smart home and AV service pages, this is a significant missed opportunity. The Corka Bridge House Control4 installation and the LCASC project are exactly the kind of evidence-based content that converts affluent buyers — "I can see what they do, and it looks like what I want."

Real photos of completed Control4 installs, Hikvision CCTV deployments, and professional electrical work would:
- Replace placeholder images that signal unfinished content to Google
- Provide genuine E-E-A-T signals (actual experience, not stock imagery)
- Improve conversion rate on high-value service pages (smart home, AV, security)
- Give the /projects/ page substantive content worth indexing

Professional photography does not need to be expensive. A photographer for half a day at one or two premium installs would yield enough images for all service pages. Until then, high-quality smartphone photography of actual completed work is better than any placeholder.
